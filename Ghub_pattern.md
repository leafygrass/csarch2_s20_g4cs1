## 1. Introduction

  This Intel-based entry-level gaming PC build is designed for gamers who want a premium experience with excellent performance at 1440p and solid capability at 4K, all while staying within a controlled budget. It combines a powerful Intel processor with a high-value graphics card, ensuring smooth gameplay in demanding modern titles. Alongside this, the build includes fast NVMe storage, ample memory, and a reliable motherboard, all housed in a small case with strong airflow and paired with a high-quality power supply to support both stability and future upgrades.
  
  The target use case is primarily gaming at 1440p resolution, with enough power to handle many games at 4K, while also being versatile enough for general productivity, streaming, and medium content creation. The build has a budget of 100,000.00 with a small flexibility of up to 5% focused more on practicality than aesthetics, aiming to squeeze as much power and efficiency into a high-performance gaming system.

## 4. Compatibility Justification
For each part, explain compatibility and choices.  
**CPU + Motherboard**
- The **Intel® Core™ Ultra 5 245KF** uses the **LGA1700 socket**, which is supported by the A**SRock B860M Pro RS WiFi** motherboard. This ensures full compatibility without requiring BIOS updates. The board also supports DDR5 and PCIe 5.0, aligning with the CPU’s modern features.

**CPU Cooler**
- The **Thermalright Royal Pretor 130 Ultra** air cooler has a height of 125 mm, well within the clearance of the **Deepcool CH160 Plus mATX case** (supports up to 172 mm). It connects via a standard 4-pin PWM header and 3-pin ARGB header, both available on the motherboard.

**RAM**
- The **Corsair Vengeance RGB DDR5 32GB (2×16GB) 6000MHz CL36** kit is fully compatible with the motherboard, which supports DDR5 up to 7200+ MHz (OC). The dual-channel kit provides high bandwidth for gaming and productivity, with two DIMM slots still available for future upgrades.

**Storage (SSD)**
- The **Kioxia Exceria Plus G3 NVMe SSD** (PCIe Gen4, M.2 2280) works with the ASRock B860M Pro RS, which offers multiple PCIe 4.0 M.2 slots. Though released earlier, Gen4 SSDs are still widely used in 2024–2025, offering strong speed and value compared to pricier Gen5 models.

**GPU**
- The **Palit RTX 5070 Ti GamingPro S** fits within the case’s 305 mm GPU clearance (card length ~269 mm). It requires 2×8-pin PCIe connectors, which are supported by the **1STPLAYER NGDP 750W ATX 3.1 PSU**. The 750W rating exceeds the GPU’s ~300W requirement, providing sufficient headroom for transient power spikes.

**PSU**
- The **1STPLAYER NGDP 750W ATX 3.1** provides reliable power delivery, with 12VHPWR and ATX 3.1 compliance for current-gen GPUs. Its wattage is sufficient for this build and allows room for future upgrades.

**Case**
- **The Deepcool CH160 Plus mATX** supports ATX power supplies, GPUs up to 305 mm, and CPU coolers up to 172 mm. Despite its compact size, it provides the necessary clearance for all major components, making it an efficient and compatible chassis choice.

**Case Fans**
- **The Arctic P12 Slim PWM PST** was chosen for airflow optimization in a small mATX chassis. Although older, its 15 mm slim design avoids clearance issues, unlike newer but thicker 2024–2025 fans, making it the most practical choice.

## 5. Budget Analysis
- **Budget Limit**: ₱100,000  
- **Final Total**: ₱102,503.00 (❌ Over budget)  
- The build slightly exceeded the target budget due to the **high cost of the GPU (Palit RTX 5070 Ti GamingPro S)**, which took up almost half of the total budget. Other parts such as the PSU and CPU cooler were chosen for reliability and cooling efficiency rather than being the cheapest available.

**Trade-offs Considered**
- A cheaper RAM kit or SSD could have reduced the cost but at the expense of speed and future-proofing.
- The team decided to keep the 750W ATX 3.1 PSU for stability and GPU readiness, instead of going with a lower-wattage, cheaper option.
- The storage and case fans, though older releases, were selected to balance cost and practicality, ensuring the build remained functional without overspending further.

## 6. Conclusion & Learnings

This project showed how building a custom PC requires balancing **performance, compatibility, and budget**. While the PCPartPicker estimated the total price of a custom PC, local vendor prices in Manila were higher, which pushed the build slightly over budget. The group learned that availability, vendor pricing, and release year restrictions (2024–2025) significantly affect choosing PC parts.

Despite being 2.5% above budget, the final build remains **powerful, balanced, and future-proof**, capable of smooth 1440p gaming with potential for 4K. Exceptions like the 2023 SSD and case fans were justified because they offered practicality and fit within the compact case while maintaining overall performance.

The group learned the importance of thoroughly checking component compatibility, including socket type, dimensions, and power requirements. Budget planning must also account for local market prices, which may be significantly higher than international estimates. The group realized that not all parts need to be the latest release older components such as SSDs and slim case fans can still be practical, reliable, and more cost-efficient. Finally, the project highlighted that the GPU is usually the most expensive and important component of a gaming build, making it the key factor in balancing both performance and budget.
